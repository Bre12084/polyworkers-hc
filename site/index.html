Background

You Lose a Lot of Your Benefits When You Work for Yourself.

  • 01
    An Employer Insurance Subsidy

    The average employer pays $8,132 a year toward a worker's health plan. On your own, there's no employer share.

  • 02
    Help with Taxes

    Employers pay 7.65% toward your Social Security and Medicare. Self-employed, you pay the full 15.3%.

  • 03
    A retirement match

    No one adds money to your 401(k) but you.

  • 04
    Unemployment insurance

    Lose a big client, and you generally can't collect unemployment.

  • 05
    Paid leave and disability

    No automatic coverage. Only a few states let you opt in, or you buy a private policy.

Sources: KFF Employer Health Benefits Survey, 2026; IRS self-employment tax.
